\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Choosing which stored bits of a quantized network to protect is neither a single-bit problem nor an intractable one. Under a protection mask, stored-bit faults behave like Gaussian noise on every weight, set by the exact moments of its unprotected bits, plus a few discrete events in the top-order bits; passed through the network, this description reproduced measured accuracy within half a point on 18 checkpoints it was not written on. Through its drift and spread it explains what we measured on 24 checkpoints from 4,000 to 600 million weights with every stored bit allowed to fail. Ranking bits by the per-example magnitude of their gradient effect, which mixes significance tiers, beats ranking by bit position, as FORTUNE does, and by FKeras's tier-first Hessian order, and is never confirmed worse. No static ranking is guaranteed: rankings that use the sign of a flip help only at sparse faults on some models and can be worse than no protection. Mechanisms are relative in the same way: ranked triplication and codes each have models and fault rates where they are the right choice, checksum correction rarely does, and the comparison that decides between them is cheap enough to make for each design.
