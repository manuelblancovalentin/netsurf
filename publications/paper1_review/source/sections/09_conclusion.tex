\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We compared static bit rankings as equal-budget protection masks on six frozen INT8 models. Where the model is still useful, the ranking decides how far the fault rate can rise, and the per-example absolute gradient is a strong default: it holds across masks, inputs, pruned checkpoints and retrained copies, and on ResNet-8 search-based, relaxed and literature-derived allocators do not beat it. It is not universal: significance-first protection is fragile across checkpoints of one architecture, the preferred ranking reverses on an under-trained checkpoint, and protecting only flips predicted to raise the loss can be worse than no protection. At dense fault rates, triplicating ranked bits outperforms blanket codes and checksum correction of equal storage. All results assume ideal logical protection.
