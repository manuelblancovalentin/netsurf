\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
Across six frozen INT8 networks, protection quality depends on the model, task metric, BER and budget. Paired comparisons demonstrate reversals among tested rankings, while exact conditioning explains why isolated damage has no general optimality guarantee. Neither result proves that every static ranking must fail. A bounded Monte Carlo-guided comparison frequently changed the selected mask but did not establish an advantage over exact isolated selection.

The contribution is a reproducible software evaluation of these protection decisions and their limits. Reliability curves, paired effects and construction costs should support the choice of allocation for its intended operating condition, rather than a claim of a universal bit ranking.
