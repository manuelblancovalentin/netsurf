\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We compared seven static bit rankings as equal-budget protection masks on six frozen INT8 models. Where the model is still useful, the choice of ranking decides how far the fault rate can rise, and the per-example absolute-gradient ranking is a strong default among those measured, also across masks, inputs, pruned checkpoints and retrained copies. It is not universal: significance-first protection is fragile across checkpoints of one architecture, the preferred ranking reverses on an under-trained checkpoint, and a ranking that protects only flips predicted to raise the loss can be worse than none. At dense fault rates, triplicating ranked bits outperforms blanket codes and checksum correction of equal storage. All results assume ideal logical protection; how search-based or learned allocators compare under an uncertain fault rate remains open.
