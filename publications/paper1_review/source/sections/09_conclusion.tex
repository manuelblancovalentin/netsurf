\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We compared seven static bit rankings as equal-budget protection masks on six frozen INT8 models, under exact stored-bit faults, with every comparison paired on common fault fields and reported in the task metric. In the operating cells where protection can still keep a model useful, the choice of ranking is consequential, and the per-example absolute-gradient ranking is a strong default among those measured: it is never resolved worse than another ranking on the three classifiers and stays within a third of the usefulness margin on the two language models. A ranking that protects only flips predicted to raise the loss can be worse than no protection, because the faults it leaves are one-sided. Theory offers no guarantee that any static ranking is optimal across fault rates and budgets, and we do not claim to have shown empirically that none exists, nor that conditional selection is better or worse than isolated selection. The default was confirmed with masks rebuilt on new calibration draws on ResNet-8, on nine new input windows, and on DS-CNN, in a second run of eleven replications on previously used test inputs; the transformer comparisons were not repeated. On pruned ResNet-8 checkpoints the default holds; on an under-trained one the best ranking depends on the fault rate inside the useful band, which is the situation in which a fixed ranking is not enough. Apart from these ResNet-8 variants, all findings are specific to one trained checkpoint per model under ideal logical protection.
