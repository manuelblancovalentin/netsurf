\section{Discussion and Limits}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Between single bits and every combination.} The measurements fit one picture. A protection decision is made for a network in which many bits fail at once. Single-bit quantities, whether bit position, measured single-flip damage or a gradient, describe that situation only in part: single-flip effects stop adding up once a few tens of top-order bits are faulty, and three gradient scores with almost equal single-flip correlations (0.36 to 0.38 on ResNet-8) have worst regrets of 2.1, 0.06 and 3.2 points (Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}). Enumerating combinations is unnecessary as well. Two moments per weight and the few top-bit events, passed through the network, reproduce measured accuracy to about half a point on the models where we tested it, and reproduce which ranking wins at which fault rate. The effects that decide between rankings are then collective: the spread that the background of low-order faults adds to every pre-activation, and the drift that a one-sided remainder adds to all of them at once. \pending{How far this holds beyond DS-CNN and ResNet-8 is what the running confirmation will tell.}

\paragraph{What a designer can take from the measurements.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Evaluate masks in the task metric, at the intended BER and budget, with the protection mechanism's own failures included. A single-bit score is a way to propose a mask, not to judge one.
\item Do not protect by bit position alone. Significance-first protection, the basis of FORTUNE and of FKeras's tier-first order, gives up a factor of about three in tolerable BER on four of the six main-grid models, trails by 9 to 34 points on every ResNet-8 checkpoint at a moderate fault rate and by 24 points on Visual Wake Words. It is a fallback for when no calibration data exist.
\item Rank by the per-example magnitude of the gradient effect. It mixes significance tiers, as the FKeras authors anticipated a better ranking should, targets the spread and leaves no systematic drift. It is computed in seconds to minutes for the MLPerf Tiny models and was never resolved worse than another static ranking on the classifiers of the main grid, and the signed ranking never beat it on the ten further checkpoints.
\item Treat rankings that use the predicted \emph{direction} of a flip as rate-specific. They helped only on ResNet-8, only at sparse faults and only with hundreds of calibration inputs, and where they lose they lose badly, down to worse than no protection. If the fault rate is uncertain, design for the dense end: a mask built for too high a rate cost at most 0.5 to 2.6 points, one built for too low a rate up to 70.
\item Choose the mechanism with the ranking. Where faults are rare, codes leave fewer faulty bits; where they are dense, triplicating ranked bits keeps a model usable that codes and checksums do not. \pending{The all-model comparison of Run T will state where the crossover lies for each model.}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why not optimize the mask for each deployment?} For models of this size a search by fault injection, reinforcement learning or gradient-based optimization costs minutes to hours, so runtime is not the main argument for a fixed ranking. The argument is that the best mask depends on the fault rate (Sections~\ref{sec:allocation} and~\ref{sec:reversals}), which is not known when the design is committed: it varies with the radiation environment, the supply voltage, ageing and the time since the last scrub. A search answers which mask is best at the rate it assumed, and Figure~\ref{fig:wrongrate} shows what a wrong assumption costs. We tried several. On the pinned ResNet-8, Monte Carlo-guided, relaxed, group-structured and Thales- and HarDNN-style allocators (Appendix~\ref{app:search}) did not beat the absolute-gradient mask by a point where the model is usable; the relaxed optimizers lost 4 to 12 points there and the Thales and HarDNN rankings 4 to 66, while the group-structured mask, which hardware can place cheaply, lost at most one. On DS-CNN, greedy and swap searches judged by real fault injection did not beat it either, and the swaps that looked best on 32 fault fields lost their predicted gain on new fields: the search mostly fitted the noise of its own fault samples (Appendix~\ref{app:ratechoice}). Choosing among three masks by the expected rate with the two-term objective was never beaten by half a point on ResNet-8. Stochastic and learned allocators remain promising; the statistical model of Section~\ref{sec:model} gives them a cheap objective, two moments and a few events per mask instead of bit-level fault sampling, on which they could build. These are single implementations on one or two models, not evidence that learned allocators are worse in general.

\paragraph{Limits.} Each main-grid model except ResNet-8 has one trained checkpoint, and the pruned, under-trained and per-epoch checkpoints are single training runs. Calibration draws, masks and windows were varied only for ResNet-8, DS-CNN and the further checkpoints, several cohorts had been used before, and for ToyCar and the transformers the default claim remains exploratory. Masks use 64 calibration inputs except in Appendix~\ref{app:calibration}, the ranking-choice studies and Run~T, which use 1,024 (up to 8,192 for DS-CNN). The noise-plus-events model was developed on DS-CNN and the pinned ResNet-8 after their outcomes were read \pending{and is being confirmed on 18 unseen checkpoints}. The selected rate-aware mask was confirmed on ResNet-8 only and defined after its predecessor's overfitting had been seen on training-side data. \pending{The ranking comparisons of the main grid assume ideal protection; Run~T replaces them with failing redundancy on the TFLite models, and FKeras and the mechanism comparison are still to be run on ToyCar, Pythia and Qwen.} Counting protected bits ignores mask regularity: at 12.5\% the ResNet-8 absolute-gradient mask protects 59\% of sign bits, 28\% of the next tier and 10\% of the third, and its placement, metadata, voters and decoders are not priced. Biases, scales, embeddings and normalization parameters are never faulted, which protects them for free, and activation, datapath and control faults are outside the fault model. Faults are independent and persistent, logical BER is a model parameter, and the band in which masks differ, about $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-1}$, is a regime of dense memory errors that periodic scrubbing would shorten~\cite{biswas2005avf}. Gradient and Hessian scores come from differentiable companions, the DistilBERT calibration sentences were seen in fine-tuning, and token losses are capped at 30 nats, so language-model magnitudes outside the useful region are lower bounds.

\paragraph{Next steps.} The statistical model makes a protection allocator possible that chooses, for each bit or word, no protection, triplication, five-fold redundancy or a code, from the residual error curve of each mechanism and a few network passes. Pricing those choices in area and power, and calibrating the fault rate to a radiation environment, belong to a hardware study that this work prepares but does not contain.
