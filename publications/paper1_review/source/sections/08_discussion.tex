\section{Discussion and Limits}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What a designer can take from the measurements.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Judge masks in the task metric, at the intended BER and budget, with the mechanism's own failures included. A single-bit score proposes a mask; it does not judge one. Three gradient scores with almost equal single-flip correlations (0.36 to 0.38 on ResNet-8) have worst regrets of 2.1, 0.06 and 3.2 points (Appendix~\ref{app:evidence}).
\item Do not protect by bit position alone. Significance-first protection, the basis of FORTUNE and of FKeras's tier-first order, is a fallback for when no calibration data exist.
\item Rank by the per-example magnitude of the gradient effect. It mixes significance tiers, as the FKeras authors anticipated a better ranking should, targets the spread and leaves no systematic drift, and it is computed in seconds to minutes for the MLPerf Tiny models.
\item Treat rankings that use the \emph{direction} of a flip as specific to a fault rate, and when the rate is uncertain design for its dense end: a mask built for too high a rate cost at most 0.5 to 2.6 points, one built for too low a rate up to 70.
\item Choose the mechanism with the ranking: codes where faults are rare, triplication of ranked bits where they are dense. \pending{Run~T will state where the crossover lies for each model.}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why not optimize the mask for each deployment?} For models of this size a search by fault injection, reinforcement learning or gradient-based optimization costs minutes to hours, so runtime is not the main argument for a fixed ranking. The argument is that the best mask depends on a fault rate that is not known when the design is committed, and Figure~\ref{fig:wrongrate} shows what a wrong assumption costs. We also tried to optimize. On the pinned ResNet-8, Monte Carlo-guided, relaxed, group-structured and Thales- and HarDNN-style allocators (Appendix~\ref{app:search}) did not beat the absolute-gradient mask by a point where the model is usable; the relaxed optimizers lost 4 to 12 points there and the Thales and HarDNN rankings 4 to 66. On DS-CNN, greedy and swap searches judged by real fault injection did not beat it either, and swaps that looked best on 32 fault fields lost their predicted gain on new fields: the search mostly fitted the noise of its own samples (Appendix~\ref{app:ratechoice}). Stochastic and learned allocators remain promising, and the statistical model of Section~\ref{sec:model} gives them a cheap objective (two moments and a few events per mask instead of bit-level fault sampling) on which they could build. These are single implementations on one or two models, not evidence that learned allocators are worse in general.

\paragraph{Limits.} Each main-grid model except ResNet-8 has one trained checkpoint, and the pruned, under-trained and per-epoch checkpoints are single training runs. Several evaluation cohorts had been used before, and for ToyCar and the transformers the default claim remains exploratory. The noise-plus-events model was developed on DS-CNN and the pinned ResNet-8 after their outcomes were read \pending{and is being confirmed on 18 unseen checkpoints}; the selected rate-aware mask was confirmed on ResNet-8 only and defined after its predecessor's overfitting had been seen. \pending{The ranking comparisons of the main grid assume ideal protection; Run~T replaces them with failing redundancy, and FKeras and the mechanism comparison are still to be run on ToyCar, Pythia and Qwen.} Protection cost is counted in stored bits; mask regularity, voters, encoders and decoders are not priced. Biases, scales, embeddings and normalization parameters are never faulted, and activation, datapath and control faults are outside the fault model. Faults are independent and persistent, logical BER is a model parameter, and the band in which masks differ, about $10^{-4}$ to $10^{-1}$, is a regime of dense memory errors that periodic scrubbing would shorten~\cite{biswas2005avf}. Gradient and Hessian scores come from differentiable companions, and language-model losses are capped at 30 nats per token.

\paragraph{Next steps.} The statistical model makes possible an allocator that chooses, for each bit or word, no protection, triplication, five-fold redundancy or a code, from each mechanism's residual error curve and a few network passes. Pricing those choices in area and power and calibrating the fault rate to a radiation environment belong to a hardware study that this work prepares.
