\section{Discussion and Limits}\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{What a designer can take from the measurements.} First, evaluate masks in the task metric at the intended BER and budget; single-fault correlation did not predict protection quality here. Second, among cheap static rankings the per-example absolute gradient is a sound default on the measured models: it costs seconds to minutes for the MLPerf Tiny models and was never resolved worse than an alternative on the classifiers, which also held for masks rebuilt on new calibration draws on ResNet-8 and DS-CNN. Where no calibration data are available, significance-first protection is a reasonable fallback, extending the useful BER range by a factor of three to ten over no protection at 12.5\%, but on four of the six models it gives up a further factor of about three. Third, scores that select by the predicted \emph{direction} of a flip should be avoided for dense faults. They help slightly when faults are sparse and can be worse than no protection otherwise. Fourth, a significance-first policy at a partial tier needs an explicit within-tier rule: on ResNet-8 a uniform random fill of the tier was up to 18 accuracy points worse than filling tensors in storage order.

\paragraph{Why the absolute gradient is robust and the positive gradient is not.} Both use the same per-example gradients and protect a similar share of high-order bits. The absolute score ignores direction, so the faults it leaves unprotected remain balanced between loss-increasing and loss-decreasing first-order effects, as they are with no protection. The positive score removes one side. This is consistent with the general point of Section~\ref{sec:allocation}: the value of protecting a bit depends on what else is faulty, and a first-order prediction made on the clean model says little about a model receiving thousands of simultaneous flips.

\paragraph{Limits.} The evidence has clear boundaries.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{One checkpoint; confirmation on two models.} Each model has a single trained checkpoint, so variation over training runs is unmeasured. Calibration draws, masks and evaluation windows were varied only in the confirmation runs, which meet the decision rule on ResNet-8 and, in the second run, on DS-CNN. For ToyCar and the three transformers each deterministic ranking has one mask per budget, their evaluation cohorts were used in earlier exploratory runs, and the default claim remains exploratory. Exposure caveats apply to the confirmation data. The second DS-CNN run reuses test clips of the main grid and of the first run. The DS-CNN windows had also been used by earlier six-bit conditional-selection studies, though not for whole-mask comparisons. And we did not audit whether a 200-image subset used in early ResNet-8 engineering runs overlaps the new ResNet-8 windows.
\item \emph{Bit-count cost.} Counting protected bits treats a regular mask and an irregular one as equally expensive. The absolute-gradient mask is irregular: at 12.5\% on ResNet-8 it protects 59\% of sign bits, 28\% of the next tier and 10\% of the third, covering between 6\% and 63\% of the bits of individual tensors. A physical mechanism would need per-bit placement or metadata that this study does not price. Tier- or layer-structured variants were not measured.
\item \emph{Excluded state.} Biases, scales, input embeddings and normalization parameters are never faulted, which amounts to protecting them for free. Activation, datapath and control faults are outside the fault model.
\item \emph{Fault regime.} Faults are independent and persistent, and logical BER is a model parameter. The band in which masks differ in usefulness lies between about $10^{-4}$ and $10^{-1}$, which is a regime of dense memory errors, not of rare single upsets, and for the transformers even $p=10^{-5}$ means thousands of simultaneous faults. Section~\ref{sec:fault} cites one published environment in which unrefreshed storage reaches this band within hours to days; that motivates the range and is not a calibrated physical upset rate.
\item \emph{Metrics.} Token losses are capped at 30 nats. The cap is immaterial in useful conditions but hides part of the damage outside them, so language-model magnitudes there are lower bounds. ToyCar's file AUC over 64 files is not a stable measure of a heavily corrupted autoencoder.
\item \emph{Missing comparators.} FKeras was not measured on the transformers, and no PrioriFI-derived, search-based or learned allocator was measured anywhere. The comparison covers low-cost static scores only.
\item \emph{Surrogates.} Gradient and Hessian scores come from differentiable companions of the integer programs, and the DistilBERT calibration sentences were seen in fine-tuning.
\end{itemize}
