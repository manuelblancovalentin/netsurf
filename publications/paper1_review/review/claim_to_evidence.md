# Scientific claim-to-evidence matrix (TECS revision, 11 November 2026)

Paths are relative to `publications/paper1_review/` unless absolute. This matrix describes the revised manuscript *Which Stored Bits Are Worth Protecting?*. The matrix for the previous draft is in git history (commit `fdba0d0`). Two approved runs were executed on 11 November 2026 (Run A, the TFLite confirmation; Run B, the language-model cap replay); their rows are at the end of the table. For everything else no model inference was run: every number is recomputed from saved policy-field records by `scripts/tecs_grid_audit.py`, `scripts/tecs_kws_conditional_audit.py` and `scripts/tecs_residual_drift_check.py`, whose outputs are stored under `data/tecs_audit/` and hash-bound in `data/tecs_revision_provenance.json`.

All measured claims are conditional on one frozen checkpoint per model and, outside Run A, on one evaluation cohort and one constructed mask per deterministic ranking and budget.

## Evidence levels

- **Analytical:** exact under stated assumptions; not a network measurement.
- **Measured, conditional:** saved outcomes on a frozen checkpoint, executable, cohort, mask and fault law.
- **Offline engineering check:** computed from saved scores, masks and stored codes without inference.
- **Not claimed:** listed so that no reader infers it.

## Deliberately not claimed

- That a universal ranking has been disproved empirically, or that one exists.
- That absolute gradient is optimal, or better than search-based or learned allocators.
- That conditional (Monte Carlo-guided) selection is better or worse than isolated selection.
- Any statement across trained checkpoints, calibration draws or fresh cohorts.
- Any hardware cost, physical mechanism (ECC, TMR), area, power or radiation-rate result.
- Anything about `legacy_qpolargrad`; it is absent from the comparison and from the text.
