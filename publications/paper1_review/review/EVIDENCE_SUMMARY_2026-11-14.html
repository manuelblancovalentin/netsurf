NetSuRF Paper 1 · ACM TECS revision · 14 November 2026

Where the evidence stands

INT8 networks with stored-bit faults; accuracy changes in percentage points; fault rates are logical bit-error rates, not physical upset rates. The Stage 2 confirmatory analysis has not run yet: 1,186 of 2,304 correction searches are done, and the analysis is expected around 15–16 November.

The claim the evidence supports

Bit-protection rankings in the literature are fixed, but the best ranking depends on the fault rate, and the common ones can be far from the best. A cheap two-moment model predicts where the ranking changes. This is confirmed on nine ResNet-8 checkpoints. The model fails on a well-trained keyword-spotting network, and the failure has a measured cause: its estimate of how much the damage spreads is too optimistic, increasingly so at higher fault rates and on well-trained models. A fixed ranking is not a safe default; protection should be chosen for the deployment fault rate.

What it does not support: a single method that wins on every model.

Weaknesses to state openly

  • Two small models are confirmatory; DistilBERT is a single exploratory run.
  • The rate-aware selection was designed after overfitting was seen on training-side data.
  • On the DS-CNN the method is close to the best fixed ranking but not within half a point everywhere.
  • No hardware cost, area, power, or physical fault rates in this paper.

Decisions for you

  1. Framing. Is "fixed rankings are unsafe; the switch is predictable on ResNet-8; here is where and why the prediction fails" strong enough for TECS without a method that wins on every model?
  2. DistilBERT. Keep it as an exploratory result, or spend time on a confirmatory run so the large-model point carries weight?
  3. Follow-up paper. Protection chosen per layer and bit position, valued with measured flips, as the next paper's method?
